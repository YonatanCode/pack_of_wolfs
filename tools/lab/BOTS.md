# Battle Lab bots

Dev-only players for the Battle Lab. `random-legal` and `aggressive-pursuit` are pure functions of the observation and a private random stream: they never touch the game, `window`, globals, the clock or `Math.random`, and never mutate the observation. Their output is an order list for `runHeadlessTurn`, always valid under the observation (hand contents, pack budget, same-action cap, living wolves, living enemies).

## What both bots see
Only `observeBattle()` (never the game): board and terrain, active units (position, facing, health, defeated, concealed; own wolves also queue and, under commander, target), own hand, reshuffle charges, a rules summary, outcome. They do NOT see enemy intent tags, the enemy plan, pack focus, RNG state or draw weights. A concealed enemy shows its position, but both bots ignore concealed enemies as targets.

## random-legal
- **Does:** under commander, gives each living wolf a random target among visible enemies (or none); then queues 1..(remaining budget, hand size) random hand cards on random living wolves.
- **Cannot:** plan, pursue or defend on purpose; read intents; reshuffle, change movement mode or unqueue. Unchanged in L3 (same outputs for the same seed; pinned to the L2 golden by test b2).
- **Determinism:** uses only `ctx.rng()`. Same observation and same seed give identical orders.

## aggressive-pursuit
- **Targets (commander):** each living wolf targets the nearest visible enemy (Manhattan distance, ties to the lowest id; skipped when already its target). Concealed enemies are ignored.
- **Actions:** spends the remaining budget round-robin over the wolves, using only cards in the hand. Per wolf and slot: if an Attack can plausibly land (below) it takes Attack, else Defend, else Move; otherwise it takes Move (closing in), else Defend. It never queues an Attack that cannot plausibly land; budget may stay unspent. With no visible enemy it never attacks (Move, then Defend).
- **Reachability (replaces "an earlier action makes a later Attack useful"):** `movesNeeded` = the fewest Move actions for the wolf to stand adjacent (8 neighbours) to its target, searched over the observation's terrain (pond, hill, tree blocked) and every unit's current tile (blockers), with a Move covering 1 to 3 tiles in one straight line of the 8 directions (the engine's `moveActionTileCount`). 0 means adjacent now; no route means never plausible. A wolf's action in queue slot `s` resolves in tick `s`, after the Moves in its slots before `s` (cards already queued count). An Attack is plausible when: slot 0 and the target is adjacent now; or slot > 0 and `movesBefore >= movesNeeded + 1`.
- **Uncertainty (simultaneous movement):** all units move in the same tick from start-of-tick positions and the bot cannot see enemy intent, so the target may step away (or toward) while the wolf moves; collisions can also shorten a Move. The bot never predicts enemy motion. Instead it is conservative: the route is computed on current positions, and every Attack after slot 0 needs one SPARE Move beyond the route, so the wolf can follow a target that retreats about one step. A second Attack on an adjacent target with no Move in between is therefore not queued; it queues Move/Defend. This is a heuristic, not a guarantee; a fast target can still escape.
- **Reshuffle (own round):** in round 0 of a two-round turn (`ctx.round === 0`, `ctx.maxRounds >= 2`) it returns exactly `[{ reshuffle: true }]` when charges remain (`hand.reshuffleChargesRemaining > 0`), nothing is queued yet (a reshuffle discards queued cards, so queued carry-over is never thrown away), the hand is not empty, and the hand is poor. Poor means the plan this hand would produce (Moves and Attacks are "useful", Defend is filler) fails any of: an enemy is within reach (some wolf needs at most 1 Move) but the plan has no Attack; no enemy is within reach (or none is visible) and the plan has fewer than 2 Moves; fewer than min(2, budget) useful actions. Round 1 never reshuffles and plans from the new observation. A caller that passes no `ctx.round` (legacy) never gets a reshuffle.
- **Cannot:** dodge, heal, read intents, track concealed enemies, predict enemy motion, change movement mode or unqueue.
- **Determinism:** ignores `ctx.rng`; the same observation and ctx give identical orders. No mutation of the observation.

Both bots return `[]` when nothing useful is legal (empty hand, budget used up, no living wolves, nothing worth queuing).

## search-benchmark (FULL-INFORMATION BENCHMARK)
Not an observation-only bot and not a human model: a bounded one-turn-lookahead search that looks for effective LEGAL play. It reads the hidden enemy plan and concealed enemies and simulates candidate turns with the game's own rules, so **its win rate does NOT estimate how hard the game is for a human**. Reports label it "full-information benchmark" (summary `label`, a note, the viewer's strategy column).
- **Where:** `tools/lab/search.cjs`. The `bots.cjs` entry only registers the id with `fullInformation: true` and delegates to `ctx.search.decide(observation)`, which the driver provides for flagged bots only (other bots keep `ctx = { rng, round, maxRounds }`). Without `ctx.search` it throws (a recorded bot error). No `script.js` change: the search uses existing seams through `game.ev`.
- **Game access (read-only):** `state()` = live units incl. concealed, the enemy plan, terrain, hand cards, charges, budget, cap (no RNG, seed or stream position); `validate(orders)` = `validatePlayerOrders`; `evaluate(orders)` = validate first, then on a `snapshotBattleState()` copy inside `withBattleState`: the door's own per-order functions (`setUnitTarget` / `consumeAvailableAction` / `queueUnitAction` in `runInTurnCore`) and the real `runTurnCore`, drained up to and including the turn-end `recovery` step. Returns only units after the turn (id, team, row, col, health, defeated).
- **Candidates:** every split of CURRENT hand cards over the living wolves (each wolf an ordered sequence appended to its queue), total <= the pack's remaining budget, at least one card unless cards are already queued (the hand multiset enforces `maxSameAction`, a draw cap); under commander target selection every wolf whose queue would hold a Move or Attack gets each living enemy (concealed included) as target (an order only when it changes the target). Fixed enumeration order (wolves P1..P3, actions Attack, Move, Defend); internal pool cap 60000 (`limitHits.generation`).
- **Ranking (order only):** a static score per candidate (likely hits from `movesNeeded` on current positions, Moves that close in, Defend when an enemy planning an Attack is within 4 tiles, +50 per enemy whose likely damage reaches its health), ties by enumeration order; the top `maxCandidates` are kept and evaluated in that order. Every evaluated candidate passes the real validator first.
- **Score (decides):** lexicographic, higher is better: `[win, notLoss, wolvesAlive, enemiesKilled, damageDealt - damageReceived, -proximity, -cardsUsed]`. win = all enemies defeated with a wolf alive; damage from health before / after the turn (after this turn's recovery); proximity = sum over living wolves of the Chebyshev distance to the nearest living enemy after the turn (both sides' moves); cardsUsed = new action orders (unplayed cards carry over). Equal scores keep the higher-ranked candidate. Not scored: the next enemy plan and the post-turn hand (never computed, see below).
- **Information rules:** may read the current hidden enemy plan. Never reads the hand stream, never evaluates a reshuffle (`evaluate` refuses one), never sees the refilled hand or the next plan: the copy stops before the turn-end hand draw, and its hand stream is replaced by a fixed neutral seed anyway (s2 shows the result does not change when the live stream moves, and that no card is drawn anywhere during a search).
- **RNG finding:** the only gameplay RNG is the hand stream (`handRandom`: hand draws at the turn end and on a reshuffle). Turn resolution (moves, swings, damage, replans, recovery) is deterministic given the state, the orders and the (frozen, under commander) enemy plan. So an evaluation predicts the turn's combat exactly (s2 / s4 compare it with the real turn) and reveals the hidden enemy intent, which this benchmark is allowed to read; it reveals no future draw. Cosmetic `Math.random` is never called by a search (s2 spy).
- **Reshuffle (own round, current hand only):** in round 0 of a two-round turn, with charges left, nothing queued and a non-empty hand, it reshuffles when its best evaluated current-hand plan is poor: not a win, no kill, no damage dealt, and proximity not reduced. It returns exactly `[{ reshuffle: true }]`; the driver applies it and calls again with `round: 1`, where it searches the newly observed hand and never reshuffles.
- **Limits (per decision, from the manifest's `searchLimits`, all required):** `maxCandidates` (ranked candidates kept), `maxEvaluations` (evaluate calls, failed ones included), `maxMsPerDecision` (wall clock through the driver's `now()`, checked before each evaluation). Hitting one keeps the best so far; with nothing evaluated the fallback is the highest-ranked candidate the real validator accepts (deterministic); with no legal candidate it returns `[]`. Counts per turn slot are in `perTurn[i].search` (decisions, candidatesGenerated, candidatesKept, evaluated, rejectedByValidator, engineErrors, limitHits, fallbacks, reshuffles) and summed per strategy in the report; wall-clock ms go to the report's `nonHashed.searchMs` (not hashed). A decision that hit the time limit depends on the machine's speed; its recorded orders still replay exactly.
- **Determinism:** ignores `ctx.rng`; the same live state and limits give the same orders unless the time limit is hit.

## `legalOrderCheck(observation, orders)`
Mirrors `validatePlayerOrders` over the observation alone (same reasons, same order, same sequence rules), including `{ reshuffle: true }`: `malformed-order`, `no-reshuffle-charges` (charges counted in sequence), and `hand-not-observed` for any card order after a reshuffle in the same list; a reshuffle also empties the hand and queues for later checks in the list.

An intent-reader bot is deferred: the observation deliberately omits enemy intent tags.
